Home-practice packet

Word lists for 19 sounds, sorted by where the sound sits in the word, and a one-page guide for parents. Print only the pages you need.

How to use it

  1. Print the parent guide (the next page) and the page for the child's sound.
  2. Circle the position you're working on: beginning, middle, end, blends or vocalic R.
  3. Ask the family to tick a box each day they practice, and bring the sheet back.

What's inside

Want this to happen every day, without the paper?

Sona sends every kid home with a few minutes of daily practice that listens to each try, and shows you who practiced. speaksona.com/for-slps

The words in this packet are the ones children practice in the Sona app. Use your clinical judgment about which sound and position a child is ready for.

For parents

Practicing speech sounds at home

A few minutes most days is plenty. It should feel like a game.

  1. Pick one quiet time each day — after breakfast, in the car, before bath.
  2. Use the words your speech therapist circled. Practice only that sound, in that part of the word.
  3. Say one word clearly, then let your child say it back. Just the word on its own, with nothing added in front of it.
  4. Listen for a real try. A try is your child saying the word out loud. Pointing or staying quiet doesn't count yet — that's fine, just say the word again.
  5. Praise the try, not only the ones that sound right: "Great trying!" counts as much as "You got it!"
  6. If a word is tricky, go back to an easier one — and always finish on a word your child gets right.
  7. Tick a box for each day you practice, and bring the sheet to your next session.

If practice turns into a struggle

Stop for the day and try again tomorrow. Short and happy beats long and frustrated.

This guide supports the plan your child's speech therapist makes. It is not an evaluation. From Rachel, Sona's co-founder and a licensed pediatric speech-language pathologist.

For speech therapists

Home-practice packet

Word lists for every sound, sorted by where the sound sits in the word, a one-page guide for parents, and a tick-box tracker on every page.

How to use it

  1. Choose what the child is ready for: the sound, and the place in the word. You know the child; this packet doesn’t choose for you.
  2. Circle it on that sound’s page: beginning, middle or end of the word, or blends and vocalic R where a sound has them.
  3. Send it home with the parent guide on the next page.
  4. Ask to see the ticks at the next session.

Readiness comes first

In the Sona app, a sound is offered from about the age most children say it: P, B and M by about 3; N, T, D, K, G and F by about 4; S by about 5; V, Z, SH, CH, J, L and TH by about 6; R and TH (v) by about 7. These ages are approximate and references vary. The app also holds blends and vocalic R back for ages 2 to 4. Your clinical judgment comes first.

For parents

Practicing at home

A few minutes a day helps your child use their sound between speech sessions.

  1. Pick one quiet time a day. A few minutes is enough.
  2. Practice only what your child’s speech therapist circled: the sound, and the place in the word.
  3. Say one word on its own, clearly. Your child says it back. One word at a time, with nothing added before or after it.
  4. A try is your child saying the word out loud. Silence or pointing isn’t a try.
  5. Praise the try, not just the words that come out right.
  6. If a word is tricky, go back to an easier one, and always finish on a word your child gets right.
  7. Tick a box each day you practice, and bring the page to the next session.

This guide supports the plan your child’s speech therapist makes. It is not an evaluation. Questions about your child’s speech? Ask their speech therapist.

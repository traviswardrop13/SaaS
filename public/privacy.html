Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 24, 2026

Sona ("Sona," "we," "us") helps children practice their speech sounds at home, alongside their speech therapy. We built Sona for kids, so protecting their privacy — and giving parents control — is central to how it works. This policy explains what we collect, why, and the choices you have.

For parents & guardians. Sona is intended to be set up and managed by a parent or guardian. A child should only use Sona with a parent's permission and involvement. You can review or delete your child's information at any time by emailing privacy@speaksona.com.

Information we collect

  • Account & setup details — a parent's email address (for the account and subscription) and the information you choose to add to personalize practice, such as a child's first name and age, and the target speech sounds they're working on.
  • Practice activity — which games and sounds are practiced, how many attempts, how many the app judged a match, streaks and progress, so it can show progress and adapt the level. This is counted on the device; what leaves it is described below.
  • Voice recordings — when a child practices, Sona records short audio clips of practice for local playback. The sound checks run on the device; Sona does not receive these clips.
  • Optional names you provide — if you add names of family members or friends to personalize daily stories, that text is used to generate those stories.
  • Payment information — handled by our payment processor (Stripe) when a parent buys Premium on our website, or a clinician buys Caseload Premium for their caseload. We do not receive or store full card numbers. Premium bought inside the iPhone or iPad app is handled by Apple.
  • An invite email from your clinician — if your child’s speech-language pathologist typed your email address when adding your child, we use it once, to email you their Sona link. We do not keep it: it is not stored, not added to any email list, and not written to our logs.
  • Basic device/usage data — standard technical information (e.g., app version, general device type) needed to run and troubleshoot the service.

How we use information

  • To provide speech practice — model the sound, judge on the device whether what was said matched it, give feedback, and track practice.
  • To personalize practice (the right sounds, level, and friendly encouragement).
  • To create the progress summary you can choose to share with your speech-language pathologist.
  • To operate, secure, and improve the service, and to provide customer support.
  • To manage subscriptions and process payments.

How voice recordings are handled

Sona checks practice sounds on the phone. The mic listens during practice and optional voice-enabled games. Sona never uploads your child's recordings or uses a cloud service to check their speech.

The app may keep one clear practice try per child per day in the device's own storage so a grown-up can listen back. It stays on that device. A grown-up can choose to share a saved clip using the phone's share sheet; the selected app or recipient then handles that copy. Clearing the app's data removes the local clips.

Sona does not collect children's recordings for advertising, sell them, or use them to build advertising profiles.

Sharing with your child’s speech-language pathologist

If your child’s speech-language pathologist (SLP) gave you a link to Sona, opening it connects your family to that clinician. Sona’s free version — daily practice and free games — costs you nothing either way, and if that clinician’s caseload has Premium, your family gets every game through their link. Sharing your child’s practice with that clinician is a separate question, asked on its own: saying no costs you nothing, and it does not decide whether your family gets Premium through the link.

If you say yes, that one clinician’s dashboard shows: your child’s first name and age as you typed them, which sounds they are practicing, which days they practiced and how many tries each day, and a rough pass rate per sound. They can also send practice assignments and a short note to this app. Never any audio — this progress-sharing connection does not send recordings. Nothing about your child goes to any other clinician, and no clinician ever sees another family.

How to stop. Open the grown-ups’ area and choose “Stop sharing with your speech therapist”. Their dashboard forgets your child immediately: first name, age, practice history and assignments are deleted, not hidden, and their device is marked so later practice cannot rebuild the record. Your child’s own progress stays on your device, and the free version stays free. A clinician can also remove a child from their own caseload, which deletes the same information.

How long it is kept. Until one of you deletes it, or automatically about 150 days after the clinician’s caseload last received any practice — whichever comes first. (That timer is set on the caseload as a whole, so a child who stops practicing while classmates continue is kept until the caseload itself goes quiet. We are narrowing this to a per-child timer.)

What a clinician sees is practice, not an assessment. A “pass rate” means the phone judged that what it heard had the shape of the target sound — not that the whole word was produced correctly. The dashboard says so on every screen that shows one.

Service providers we share data with

We share limited information with trusted providers only so the service can function:

  • Text-to-speech — turns the practice words and prompts into the spoken voice you hear. (We send the words to be spoken, not your child's recordings.)
  • Story & coaching generation (AI) — generates practice stories and coaching tips; this may include a child's first name or names you've added, to personalize the story.
  • Payments — Stripe processes what is bought on our website: a family’s Premium, and a clinician’s Caseload Premium. Stripe receives the buyer’s email address and payment details, and nothing about a child.
  • In-app purchases — Premium bought inside the iPhone or iPad app is sold and billed by Apple, and RevenueCat checks with Apple whether that purchase is active, under a random identifier. Neither is given a child’s name.
  • Hosting — Vercel runs the site and its server routes.
  • Data store — Upstash holds clinician accounts, the caseload information described above, clinician sign-in sessions, and a copy of Stripe’s answer about whether a clinician’s Caseload Premium is active. It never holds audio.
  • Email delivery — Resend delivers a clinician’s sign-in link, and the one invite email a clinician can ask us to send a parent. These emails never go to children and never contain a child’s name. A parent’s address is given to Resend only to deliver that one email; Sona keeps no copy of it.
  • Email list — Kit sends the occasional email a grown-up agreed to when they gave us their address. It holds that email address, a clinician’s own first name, and whether the person is a speech therapist, a parent or something else — never anything about a child. Unsubscribe from any of its emails.
  • Analytics — PostHog, on grown-up pages only, as described below.

These providers may only use the information to perform their service for Sona.

If something goes wrong. If we confirm a breach affecting your information we will notify affected clinicians and parents without unreasonable delay, and within 72 hours of confirming it. Where a family reached Sona through a clinician we usually hold no parent email address (only one a parent gave us themselves), so notice to those families goes through their clinician.

Analytics & advertising

We do not show advertising in Sona, and we do not allow behavioral/ad tracking of children. We never place analytics or advertising trackers on the children's practice screens — those screens talk only to our own servers, which may record anonymous usage counts (for example, that a practice session was completed, its sound and its duration) tied to a random device identifier, never to a name, email, voice, or recording. Limited, privacy-respecting analytics (PostHog, configured with session recording and autocapture disabled) run on adult-facing pages of our website and of the app — such as marketing, parent setup, and settings — to understand general usage under the same anonymous identifier. The Sona iOS app contains no advertising trackers, and no third-party analytics ever run on a child's practice screen.

Children's privacy (COPPA)

Sona is directed to children and designed to be used under a parent's or guardian's supervision. We collect only the information needed to provide speech practice, and we treat children's information accordingly:

  • A parent/guardian sets up the account and consents to Sona collecting the information described above.
  • We do not condition a child's participation on disclosing more information than is reasonably necessary.
  • We do not knowingly use children's personal information for advertising, and we do not sell it.
  • Parents can review the personal information we have about their child, ask us to delete it, and refuse further collection, by contacting us.

If you believe a child has provided us information without a parent's involvement, please contact us and we will delete it.

Your choices & rights

  • Access & deletion — email us to review or delete your or your child's information.
  • Cancel — you can cancel your subscription at any time. A clinician cancels Caseload Premium from their dashboard: Caseload Premium → Manage billing.
  • Depending on where you live, you may have additional rights over your personal information; contact us to exercise them.

Data retention & security

Your child’s practice history lives on your own device, and stays there until you delete the app or clear it in Settings — we hold no copy.

What we hold on a server is: a clinician’s account (email, name, clinic, their code) for as long as that account exists; the caseload information described above, deleted when a parent stops sharing, when the clinician removes the child or their account, or automatically about 150 days after that caseload last received practice; and a pending invite, which deletes itself 30 days after it is created if the family never opens it.

We use reasonable safeguards, including encrypted connections. No method of transmission or storage is 100% secure, but we work to protect your data.

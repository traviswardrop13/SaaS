What to know before you assign it

✓
It supplements your therapy — it doesn't replace it

Sona is a home-practice tool for carryover. It doesn't diagnose, evaluate, or set goals — you do. Think of it as the worksheet that listens.

🎧
What the check can and cannot tell you

Sona decides on-device whether what it heard has the acoustic shape of the target sound. That is a narrower question than whether the word was produced correctly, and it is not a substitute for your ear — read the numbers as practice volume with a rough pass rate attached, not as a score. It will never call a wrong sound perfect and it will never count silence as a rep.

🗣️
Accent & dialect

The on-device check is tuned to General American English. It may mark features of regional, dialectal, or bilingual speech (e.g. AAVE or Spanish-influenced English) as misses — please use your clinical judgment when choosing targets and when reading the pass rate.

📅
Pick developmentally-appropriate targets

Assign sounds the child is ready for — e.g. vocalic R isn't typically expected until ~age 7. Sona practices the targets you set; it doesn't decide readiness.

🔒
How a child's audio is handled

No audio ever leaves the device. There is no cloud scorer and no third-party listener — every check happens on the phone itself, and there is no mechanism in the app that could upload a clip. One practice clip a day may be kept in the phone's own storage so a parent can listen back; it is never sent anywhere. First name only, and a parent sets the child up and consents. Nothing here for clinic IT to review.

What your IT or compliance reviewer will ask, answered

🎙️
Is a child’s voice recorded or sent anywhere?

No. Every check happens on the child’s own phone, and there is no route in the product that accepts audio — not a setting that is switched off, an absence of any mechanism. One clip a day may be kept in that phone’s own storage so a parent can listen back; it stays there.

✍️
Who gives Sona the child’s name — me, or the parent?

The parent, on their own device, after consenting. When you add a child ahead of time you type initials or a nickname, and Sona discards even that once the family joins. You never send Sona a student’s name, which is what keeps this a parent-initiated disclosure rather than a school record shared with a vendor.

📋
What is stored, and where?

On our server: your account (email, name, clinic, your code), and for each consenting family the child’s first name and age as the parent typed them, the sounds being practised, practice days and counts, a pass rate per sound, and the assignments you set. Hosted on Vercel; stored in Upstash; sign-in links sent by Resend; analytics (PostHog) on grown-up pages only, never on a child’s screen. No advertising, no sale of data, no profiling.

🗑️
How is it deleted?

Three ways, all immediate: the parent taps “stop sharing” in the app; you remove the child from your caseload; or it ages out about 150 days after your caseload last received practice. Removal is a real delete, and the child’s device is marked so later practice cannot rebuild the record. A pending invite nobody opens deletes itself after 30 days.

📄
Do you sign agreements?

Ask us — we will answer in writing, and we would rather have the conversation before you hand the link to a family than after. Our position on a BAA: the family shares their data with us directly, after consenting on their own phone, rather than the clinic sharing it on their behalf. If we confirm a breach we notify affected clinicians and parents within 72 hours of confirming it; families who reached us through you are reached through you, since we hold no parent email on this channel.

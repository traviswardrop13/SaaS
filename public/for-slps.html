What to know before you assign it

✓
It supplements your therapy — it doesn't replace it

Sona is a home-practice tool for carryover. It doesn't diagnose, evaluate, or set goals — you do. Think of it as the worksheet that listens.

🎧
What the check can and cannot tell you

Sona decides on-device whether what it heard has the acoustic shape of the target sound. That is a narrower question than whether the word was produced correctly, and it is not a substitute for your ear — read the numbers as practice volume with a rough pass rate attached, not as a score. It will never call a wrong sound perfect and it will never count silence as a rep.

🗣️
Accent & dialect

The on-device check is tuned to General American English. It may mark features of regional, dialectal, or bilingual speech (e.g. AAVE or Spanish-influenced English) as misses — please use your clinical judgment when choosing targets and when reading the pass rate.

📅
Pick developmentally-appropriate targets

Assign sounds the child is ready for — e.g. vocalic R isn't typically expected until ~age 7. Sona practices the targets you set; it doesn't decide readiness.

🔒
How a child's audio is handled

No audio ever leaves the device. There is no cloud scorer and no third-party listener — every check happens on the phone itself, and there is no mechanism in the app that could upload a clip. One practice clip a day may be kept in the phone's own storage so a parent can listen back; it is never sent anywhere. First name only, and a parent sets the child up and consents. Nothing here for clinic IT to review.

One app, two ways in.

For parents
  1. Set up in about a minute. Three quick questions on your phone, then your child plays.
  2. A few minutes of practice a day. Echo asks for the sound, listens, and a game comes after.
  3. Watch the days add up. A weekly goal and a streak, right in the app.
For speech therapists
  1. Set the target. Pick the sound, the position and a daily goal.
  2. Send the family link. Copy the ready-made message. Parents set up their child in about 30 seconds.
  3. Walk in prepared. Practice days, reps and pass rate by sound and position.

The part kids actually ask for.

A daily adventure with Echo, games to play after practice and coins to earn: free games, the released library with Premium. The fun is what brings kids back, so the practice keeps happening at home.

Good to know before you start.

Sona is practice that listens. It supports speech therapy; it doesn't diagnose, evaluate or set goals.

Read it as practice volume

The check asks whether a sound has the acoustic shape of the target. That's narrower than your ear. Treat pass rate as a rough signal, not a score.

Tuned to General American English

Dialectal or bilingual speech, like AAVE or Spanish-influenced English, may be marked as misses. A speech therapist's judgment comes first.

Readiness comes first

Sona holds a sound back until a child is typically old enough for it: vocalic R, for example, isn't expected until about age 7. An SLP assigns what each child is ready for.

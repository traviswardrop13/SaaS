How it works

A few minutes a day. Nothing for you to plan.

Say… rain.
1

Echo says a word.

One word at a time, for the sound your child is working on. Your child hears it first, then gets a turn.

2

Your child says it back.

The phone listens right on the device. A try counts only when it hears your child’s voice, so silence never counts.

3

Practice unlocks the fun.

Practice earns tickets for games and coins to spend. If a word is tricky, Sona makes it easier, so every round ends on a win.

Made with a speech therapist

Made with someone who does this every day.

Rachel Wardrop, MS · Co-founder · Licensed pediatric speech-language pathologist

Rachel is Sona’s co-founder and a licensed pediatric speech-language pathologist who loves helping children build confidence in communication. She sets the rules Sona keeps:

  • Start simple.Practice begins with one sound and moves up to words and sentences as your child is ready.
  • Silence never counts.A try counts only when the phone hears your child’s voice.
  • End on a win.If a word is hard, Sona steps it down, so the last try is a success.
  • The right sound at the right age.Sona waits on a sound until a child is typically old enough for it.
Safe by design

Your child’s voice stays on your phone.

Every try is checked right on the device. There is no cloud listener, and the app never sends your child’s voice anywhere.

Sona is free to start: daily practice and free games, with no card.

Kids about 3 to 8. Sona holds back a sound until a child is typically old enough for it: the R sound, for example, isn’t expected until about age 7.

19 speech sounds: P, B, M, N, T, D, K, G, F, V, S, Z, SH, CH, J, L, R, and both TH sounds (as in “thumb” and “this”).

The phone listens for the shape of the sound, right on the device. It’s a quick check that keeps practice honest, not a test: silence never counts, and every round ends on a win. It’s tuned to American English, so some accents may be asked to try again more often.

No. Sona is practice at home. It doesn’t diagnose, evaluate or set goals. If you’re worried about your child’s speech, a speech-language pathologist is the right person to ask, and if your child already sees one, Sona is a fun way to practice between visits.
